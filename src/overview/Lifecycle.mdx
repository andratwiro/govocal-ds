# Lifecycle

Every part sits at one of three stages. The stage says how much you can lean on it: whether its look, its props and
its behaviour will stay as they are.

{/* A table goes through the Markdown block: MDX here has no GitHub tables. */}
<Markdown>{[
  "| Stage | What it means | What it promises | What it takes to move on |",
  "|---|---|---|---|",
  "| **Draft** | Built, but its shape is still being worked out. | Nothing yet: props, look and behaviour may change in any update. Fine in the demo back office, not in a product screen. | Every state has a story, it works in both directions, its keyboard behaviour is tested, and its page is written. |",
  "| **Candidate** | Complete as far as design can take it. | Its look and behaviour hold. Its props may still change after a developer's review, and each change is written in `CHANGELOG.md`. | A developer has reviewed its API and it is used in at least one real screen. |",
  "| **Stable** | Reviewed by both sides. | Props are not renamed or removed without notice: a change that breaks a screen comes with a note on how to move. | Stays stable until it is replaced; then it is marked deprecated. |",
].join('\n')}</Markdown>

A part on its way out is **deprecated**: the sidebar says so after its name, and its page names what to use instead.

## Where things stand

- **Nothing is stable yet.** A part becomes stable only once a developer has reviewed its API, and no review has
  happened ([Who owns what](?path=/docs/overview-who-owns-what--docs)).
- **Not in Figma doesn't hold a part back.** A part built from the prototype alone can still be a candidate: Figma
  drawing it is a separate question (`docs/OPEN-DECISIONS.md`).
- Each part's stage shows on its page (under Status), after its name in the sidebar, and in the sidebar's tag filter
  (the `status:` tags).

## How a part's stage is decided

A part is any entry under Components or Patterns; Base parts have no stage (they are primitives with a one-line page,
see [Base](?path=/docs/base-introduction--docs)). The stage comes from facts in the repo, never from a feeling, so
anyone running `npm run lifecycle` gets the same answer:

- **Stable** when a developer has reviewed its API. None yet.
- **Candidate** when all three hold:
  - **its shape is settled outside its own stories**: Figma draws it, or two or more back-office files use it;
  - **its behaviour is proven**: it takes no keys or presses, or a test of its own presses it (a story's play function,
    or a browser test that opens one of its stories);
  - **its page is written** (every part's is).
- **Draft** otherwise: a part only its stories use, or used once in the back office and not drawn in Figma, or one
  that takes keys with no test of its own.

So a Not in Figma part used across the back office is a candidate, and a part Figma draws but nothing tests yet stays
a draft. `npm run lifecycle` prints how many parts sit at each stage.

## Who moves a part

The facts move a part from draft to candidate: once Figma draws it or a second back-office screen uses it, and a test
presses it, `npm run lifecycle` retags it (ask an agent: *"Rerun the lifecycle tags."*). A developer's API review moves
it to stable: the part goes in `REVIEWED` in `scripts/lifecycle.mjs` with the review's date. Either side can change
the rule, with a line in `docs/DECISIONS.md` saying why.
